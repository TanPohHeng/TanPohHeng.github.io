Why I like Melty Blood Type Lumina

Written by

Poh Heng

in

Game Design Breakdown, Hobbies

A screenshot of Melty Blood Type Lumina (MBTL)

Melty Blood Type Lumina (MBTL) is a game in the fighting game genre, specifically, an anime fighter. Like other games in the fighting games space, MBTL ‘s combat system is quite intricate, requiring players to make multiple split second decisions to come out on top.

So how do you introduce these systems/intricacies to a player who has never played a fighting game before? How do you get someone who may not have the patience of a committed fighting game veteran to continue playing beyond their first match, win or lose? With the fighting games space being notoriously difficult to enter and user retention rates generally lower than other game genres, how does MBTL attempt to keep newer players invested in learning how to play fighting games?

In my views, MBTL attempts to answer these questions through these 3 mechanics:

  • Rapid Beat (Auto combo pickups)
  • Shield (Universal parry)
  • Forced Release (Heat system)

We will look at how the Rapid Beat mechanic tries to tackle theses questions for this post.

Rapid Beat

Rapid beat is an auto combo mechanic that allows players to cancel any normals/buttons/attacks into an attack sequence that the opponent cannot escape from if hit by any part of the sequence.

Without going in depth on MBTL’s rebeat system (a system that allows normals/buttons/attacks to cancel into one another following certain rules), whenever multiple attacks are performed in a sequence using the rebeat system, reusing any previously used attack in the same sequence automatically activates Rapid Beat.

Any subsequent attacks outside of special moves or Arc Drives simply causes the Rapid Beat to automatically continue the combo until it is completed. You can see this in action at 0:26 of the video above where the combo consist of two different buttons before continuing to a Rapid Beat.

In the following sections, I’ll cover some of the reasons I think Rapid Beat helps beginners.

Interactions to win

Problem:

One of the most common issue for beginners in fighting games is not knowing how to convert hits into high damage combos. This in turn raises the number of interactions the beginner has to win in a round to actually win the round.

In MBTL, every character has 11,001 hp. This roughly converts to about 2.75 combos if each combo dealt around 4000 damage, which is about the average damage for a optimized combo for most characters if they use 1 meter of their magic circuit(the super meter for MBTL).

Only 3 interactions are required for an experienced player to win a round given the right circumstances.

On the other hand, a stray hit from any button or attack usually hits for less than 1000 damage, with special moves generally doing around 1000 to 1500 damage and Arc Drives doing 3500 damage. This can roughly translates to beginners needing to win up to 11 ~ 15 interactions or more to take a round, especially if all damage they do comes from their buttons.

Beginners can take more than 10 interactions to win a round from stray hits.

This disparity in the number of interactions a player needs to win in order to take a round between a seasoned player and a beginner is can make the odds of a beginner winning near insurmountable, especially when taking into consideration that most combos end with more potential damage in the form of okizeme(scenarios where a player is knocked down and has to stand up and defend from attacks at a disadvantage).

Approach:

The way MBTL approaches this disparity through its Rapid Beat system, which allows beginners to perform basic combos of any button that do around 2000 to 2500 damage and lead to generally advantageous okizeme scenarios.

Basic conversion for all Saber’s buttons using Rapid Beat results in a combo of around 2000 to 2500 damage and lead to an advantageous knockdown position.

In essence, the Rapid Beat mechanic lowers the required interactions to win a round for a beginner from 11 ~ 15 to just 5 ~ 6 interactions. It also allows almost any stray hit to be converted into an advantageous scenario without any prior training, ever so slightly evening the playing field for a beginner.

Although Rapid Beat may allow very easy conversion of any hit into a 2000~2500(5~6 interactions to win) damage combo, it should be noted that any player who puts in time to learn a basic combo route can easily deal at least 3000 damage (4 interactions to win) from any hit. In that sense, Rapid Beat can help a beginner get basic damage off any hit, but it always leaves more room for the player to improve through training.

For players only dipping their toes into fighting games, they won’t need to spend time learning basic combo routes like in other fighting games and step into their first battle to have fun without too great of a disadvantage. If anything, this might help them realize that the gap between a newbie and intermediate player is not as wide as they believe, and hopefully encourage them to continue playing.

More motivated players will likely try to learn better combos that do more damage either through the in-game combo trials mode or from the internet. For them, Rapid Beat acts more like a safety device, one mechanic they can fall back on while learning to perform harder combos that do more damage.

On Block

Problem:

In MBTL, block strings last for as long as the player can rebeat their normals. For most newcomers to fighting games, learning what to do on hit or block is not a skill that comes naturally. Sometimes, they may not even realize when they have put the opponent into a block string.

Being able to force a favorable scenarios based on if your opponent is hit or blocks is one way to keep the advantage when pursuing your own offense, but this is hard to achieve as a beginner as these scenarios usually require a strict combination of moves to achieve.

A sample block string that transitions into a basic combo on hit, or a spacing trap on block. A extended version of it can be seen here.

Approach:

Rapid Beat ends differently depending on if it hits, or is blocked. The difference in the opponent’s hit reaction on such a lengthy animation makes it easy for beginners to easily tell if the move was a hit or was blocked.

Rapid Beat has different outcomes based on if it hits or is blocked, even though the input is the same for both scenarios.

Since Rapid Beat has different result outcomes based on if it hits or is blocked, even though the input is the same for both scenarios, beginner players can easily tell if the opponent has blocked and pivot their attacks based on the situation.

On Block, the last hit of a Rapid Beat is actually punishable. This means if not cancelled into a safe special move, the player performing the Rapid Beat can be hit(punished), without being able to block.

Because losing health from being punished while trying to start your offense is basically giving the round away to the opponent for free, canceling an unsafe attack into a safe special move is an important learning point for many fighting games. Rapid Beat’s lengthy animation makes it an ideal place to practice this concept.

When examining the block string a player can use with and without Rapid Beat, you can notice that block strings without Rapid Beat essentially achieves the same results as those without it. Both end in a scenario where the opponent cannot deal damage that cannot be blocked. Though there are nuances as to differences between both scenarios, it won’t be more important to a beginner player than not losing health for trying to attack.

A block strings without using Rapid Beat, and one using Rapid Beat. Notice the advancing player can block any retaliation from the opponent after both strings.

Beyond Beginner Play

Rapid Beat is a mechanic mostly used to bridge the number of interactions needed to win between beginner and intermediate players by allowing conversions off nearly any button, and as an introduction/training tool for the concept block strings and hit confirming combos for beginners. Beyond these, Rapid Beat can also be a tool for advanced players as well.

Clutching Hit confirms

Like its use for beginners, Rapid Beat can be use to convert an unexpected hit into a full combo conversion. These combos usually do less damage than proper combo conversions, but it has the upside of being easy to do and more than likely ending in a strong position at the end of the combo. In scenarios where it might be nearly or actually impossible for a player to react to an unexpected hit, Rapid Beat can be used to as a low risk option to attempt to convert the hit into more damage and a better offensive position.

Cleaning your execution

As Rapid Beat only ever occurs when reusing a button in a rebeat chain, any sloppy inputs during certain combos will disrupt it with a Rapid Beat. This really incentivizes players to be aware of the inputs they use to achieve the intended move. As Rapid Beat reduces the amount of damage the opponent takes as compared to the optimal combo routing, it is ideal to ensure your combo has as few Rapid Beats as possible. Therefore, when attempting to improve your combo damage, you are incentivized to execute only the inputs required to do the combo, and not more.

Rapid Beat makes this combo easier to perform, but it does around 50 less damage than the combo without Rapid Beat.

On another note, it is actually possible to turn off or change the input requirements for Rapid Beat in MBTL. This allows players to use Rapid Beat more intentionally by removing the rebeat requirement, allowing players to play more intentionally and simplifying some combo execution requirements.

Combo Extension

Like every other attack in a character’s move list, Rapid Beat can be simply considered another attack with some unique properties. As such, it can have it’s own value in combos like every other attack. There are some combo routes that are easier or are made possible simply because Rapid Beat exists.

Rapid Beat makes this combo conversion works. Without it, the opponent is too far for the uppercut to work.

In Conclusion

Though Rapid Beat is an auto-combo mechanic that some fighting game players my put off as a clutch for beginner players who do not know how to execute combos, I believe that the developers had planned the design of the mechanics to introduce new players to the intricacies of fighting games as a whole.

Rapid Beat help raise the odds for a beginner to win to a competitive level, allowing even beginners to have fun taking on intermediate level players. It also acts as backup training wheels for more motivate players who aspire to perform harder combos.

Rapid Beat also introduces the concept of ending your offense when blocked on a safe move. With its lengthy animation, it acts as an easily recognizable stop sign for beginner to check if their attacks are blocked and perform action appropriate when that scenario occurs.

Rapid Beat has its uses beyond beginner level play and can act as a tool for players beyond that skill level develop cleaner execution, convert from unexpected stray hits and to extend combos beyond what is possible without it.

By providing the Rapid Beat mechanic for players in MBTL, the developers have provided new players a way to have fun without committing to MBTL, learn combos and block strings with a safety net to fall back on, and provided the tools for more motivated players to keep learning how to play MBTL.

This attention to assisting player’s progression through a single mechanic is exactly why it is easy to introduce to those who don’t usually play fighting games, and why I like Melty Blood Type Lumina as a fighting game.
